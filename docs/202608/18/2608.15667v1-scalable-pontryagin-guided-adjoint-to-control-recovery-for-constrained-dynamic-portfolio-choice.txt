Abstract 

We develop a scalable adjoint-to-control framework for continuous-time portfolio choice and consumption under smooth pointwise constraints. A feasible direct-policy-optimization (DPO) policy supplies rollouts; after training, its latent controls are detached before the state-dependent feasible chart is applied. Once- and twice-differentiated continuation payoffs on this fixed-latent open-loop BPTT (OL-BPTT) graph are conditionally projected to the vector first adjoint and matrix second adjoint, while a nested antithetic common-random-number regression estimates the shifted wealth-row martingale input. Conditional on the estimated adjoint tuple, deployment solves the local generalized-Hamiltonian problem by an exact QP for quadratic-affine blocks and by a log barrier otherwise. We prove an OL-BPTT–PMP correspondence retaining orthogonal projection residuals, a local barrier–KKT approximation, and a performance-to-adjoint bridge under local quadratic growth. In an 

n = 100 borrowing-allowed no-short-sale Merton benchmark, the learned first adjoint has mean relative error 0.46% ; under the analytical policy, the first adjoint, wealth curvature, and Brownian coefficient have normalized errors 0.031% , 0.035% , and 0.326% , respectively. In a predictable-return benchmark, the analytical ZX coefficient is recovered within 0.260% 

and 0.986% , and the corresponding decoded-policy RMSEs are 2.465 × 10 −3 and 8.429 ×

10 −3. The direct P XY –VXY comparison, reported only as a numerical diagnostic rather than a PMP ground-truth error, is 0.318% and 0.350% . Across constraint, factor, barrier, and switching-region audits, recovery sharply reduces local KKT residuals; all final barrier runs converge, and the finite-barrier gap remains approximately first order in the barrier parameter. The implementation remains practical for portfolios with up to 100 risky assets. 

Keywords: Constrained continuous-time portfolio optimization; Pontryagin’s Maximum Principle (PMP); Adjoint-to-control recovery; Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions; Open-loop backpropaga-tion through time (OL-BPTT); Large-scale portfolio control 

# 1 Introduction 

Dynamic portfolio choice—how an investor allocates wealth across assets and consumes over time—has been central to financial economics since Samuelson (1969) and Merton (1969, 1971). Classical models admit closed-form or semi-analytic solutions under frictionless trading and unrestricted borrowing or short selling, including extensions with stochastic investment oppor-tunities (e.g., Kim and Omberg, 1996; Liu, 2007). Realistic portfolios, however, face short-sale

For the constraints studied here, the feasible control fiber is known once the current state is fixed; the difficulty is therefore not a classical unknown obstacle boundary, but the nonsmooth dependence of the constrained maximizer and its active set on the state and adjoint inputs. Genuine free-boundary, reflected, or singular-control formulations arise in related problems with state constraints, transaction costs, ratcheting, or path dependence (e.g., Karatzas et al., 1987; Cvitanić and Karatzas, 1993; Karatzas and Shreve, 1998; Xu and Shreve, 1992a,b; Fleming and Soner, 2006; Dybvig, 1995; Constantinides, 1990; Sundaresan, 1989). This distinction suggests a computational decomposition. Conditional on the current state and the required first- and second-order adjoints, the pointwise control problem is finite dimen-sional and can be handled by a standard constrained solver. The global dynamic task is to obtain the wealth-relevant blocks of those adapted adjoints over the deployment region. This is particularly relevant in portfolio models whose Markov state is low or moderate dimensional while the constrained action vector is large. Grid-based dynamic programming becomes costly as the state dimension grows, whereas enlarging the asset universe separately increases the local action block (Campbell and Viceira, 1999, 2001; Campbell et al., 2003; Balduzzi and Lynch, 1999; Lynch and Balduzzi, 2000; Lynch, 2001; Brandt et al., 2005; Buraschi et al., 2010; Gar-lappi and Skoulakis, 2010; Jurek and Viceira, 2011). The present paper is organized around adjoint acquisition followed by structure-appropriate local recovery. Deep backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE) methods (Han et al., 2018; E, 2017), physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) and related neural partial differential equation (PDE) methods (Raissi et al., 2019), and simulation-based policy learning have broadened the numerical toolkit for stochastic control; recent value-based extensions also accommodate jumps (Cheridito et al., 2025). Hard-constrained PINNs (hPINNs), improved constrained PINNs (ICPINNs), and activation-based direct policy optimization (DPO) can encode simplex, nonnegativity, box, and related feasibility restrictions directly (Lu et al., 2021; Tan and Zhang, 2026; Han and E, 2016; Hu and Laurière, 2024). Feasibility, however, does not imply that the deployed action satisfies the pointwise Pontryagin maximum principle (PMP) and Karush–Kuhn–Tucker (KKT) conditions. The method of Ji et al. (2022) is closest in spirit because it combines the stochastic maxi-mum principle with deep learning; Deep Hedging (Buehler et al., 2019) and the Deep Galerkin Method (DGM) (Sirignano and Spiliopoulos, 2018) provide complementary policy-based and PDE-residual perspectives. To our knowledge, existing work has not emphasized adapted first-and second-order costate acquisition as the organizing bottleneck for large pointwise-constrained control blocks. We build on the DPO/backpropagation-through-time (BPTT) policy-training paradigm of Huh et al. (2025). New to the present paper are fixed-latent open-loop BPTT adjoint harvesting, conditional projection to adapted adjoint inputs, an anchored nested estimator of the shifted martingale input, and a solver-neutral constrained recovery stage. The theory starts from the open-loop first- and second-order stochastic Pontryagin maximum principle (PMP), before any value-function identification. Because the portfolio enters the diffusion, the first-order Hamilto-nian is linear in the portfolio; the second-order adjoint supplies the quadratic curvature required for finite control comparison and local recovery. The method has two main stages. Stage 1 has two distinct passes: an activation-based feedback DPO warm-up, followed—after the net-work is frozen—by open-loop BPTT (OL-BPTT) adjoint harvesting. In the second pass, the network-generated latent controls are stop-gradiented before the feasible-fiber chart is applied. Thus actor-state derivatives are removed from the costate variation, while the structural state derivatives of a moving feasible fiber are retained. Once- and twice-differentiated continuation payoffs with respect to the full wealth–factor state provide raw vector and matrix sensitivi-ties, and conditional projection produces the adapted adjoints. Stage 2 then solves the local generalized-Hamiltonian problem with a solver chosen for its structure. We call the log-barrier re-alization B-PGDPO and the exact local quadratic-program (QP) realization QP-PGDPO. Both 2operate on the same supplied adjoint tuple and target the same local recovery correspondence, while the finite-barrier realization retains central-path bias. In the smooth Markov setting, the first adjoint and its martingale coefficient can be identified with value derivatives, whereas the second-order PMP adjoint is not, in general, identified with the value Hessian. Accordingly, the implementation does not impose a zero-shift identity, even in the CRRA benchmarks. The analysis establishes five links in this architecture. First, regular smooth state-dependent control fibers are treated through local C2 charts on compact regions with a locally fixed reg-ular active set, with state derivatives taken at fixed latent coordinate. Second, a solver-neutral recovery map is specialized to a log-barrier central path and, in quadratic-affine blocks, to an exact QP/KKT solve without barrier bias. Third, exact discrete once- and twice-differentiated OL-BPTT identities are connected to the chart-reduced open-loop adjoint systems. The orthog-onal component of each one-step Brownian projection is retained rather than absorbed into a pointwise truncation error; we impose a Zhang-type intervalwise L2 condition directly on the relevant martingale-density families to control its aggregate energy (cf. Zhang, 2004). Fourth, a local quadratic-growth condition turns an εref -optimal DPO reference into O(√εref ) control, state, and first/second-adjoint mismatch on a common regular stratum. This endogenizes the reference-policy term in the recovery bounds and, for exact QP recovery, converts the square-root adjoint rate back into an O(εref ) integrated true local QP gap. Finally, finite-dimensional Markov augmentation is separated from approximate Markovization by adapted state compres-sion, whose error is outside the adjoint and local-solver bounds. The experiments follow a validate-then-extend design. The constrained Merton benchmark supplies analytical policies and wealth derivatives. At n = 100 , the learned first-adjoint level has mean relative error 0.46% ; under the analytical policy, the normalized errors of λX , P XX ,and ZX are 0.031% , 0.035% , and 0.326% . A one-factor affine special case of Liu (2007) then supplies analytical value derivatives, ZX , and optimal-policy targets. The analytical ZX coeffi-cient is recovered within 0.260% and 0.986% , and the corresponding decoded-policy RMSEs are 

2.465 × 10 −3 and 8.429 × 10 −3. The direct P XY –VXY discrepancies, 0.318% and 0.350% , are re-tained only as numerical diagnostics rather than PMP ground-truth errors. A three-seed budget study shows that DPO’s local residual largely plateaus, whereas exact-QP and barrier recovery remove most of the remaining Hamiltonian gap. Separate consumption-cap, constrained-factor, edge/stress, estimator, and switching-region audits check moving-fiber differentiation, shifted-input estimation, and solver behavior. The constrained Liu–Kim–Omberg experiment is retained as an adjoint-conditional stress test in Appendix G.3. Because all reported portfolio blocks are quadratic-affine once the adjoints are fixed, QP-PGDPO is the exact local-QP reference condi-tional on the common estimated adjoint tuple, and the barrier results are central-path audits, not a separate nonquadratic benchmark. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 formulates the constrained portfolio–consumption problem and reviews comparison approaches. Section 3 develops the open-loop adjoint system and the solver-neutral recovery map. Section 4 presents DPO training, OL-BPTT harvesting, and the barrier and QP deployment rules. Section 2.2 expresses the pointwise portfolio block in terms of derivatives of a smooth value function. We now formulate the same local control comparison using open-loop first- and second-order adjoints, without assuming that a globally smooth value function exists. Because the risky-asset weights enter the wealth diffusion, the relevant stochastic maximum principle is second order (Peng, 1990; Yong and Zhou, 1999). We first introduce a fixed-latent local chart and the corresponding chart-reduced adjoints, then define the generalized Hamiltonian and its portfolio specialization. We subsequently relate the adjoints to value derivatives in the smooth Markov case and formulate the constrained local recovery problem. The analysis is written for the full state S = ( X, Y ); setting dY = 0 gives the factor-free Merton specialization.

# 4 Policy Training, Fixed-Latent OL-BPTT, and End-to-End Re-covery 

Section 3 formulates constrained local recovery conditional on the adapted adjoint input ϑ =(λ, ζ, P ). This section constructs numerical estimates of that input from feasible policy rollouts and quantifies how adjoint-estimation, reference-policy, and numerical-solver errors propagate to the recovered control. Stage 1 first trains a feasible feedback policy and then freezes its latent outputs to harvest fixed-latent open-loop sensitivities. Conditional projection yields the first- and second-order ad-joints, while a separate anchored nested estimator recovers the shifted wealth-row input. We distinguish throughout between population conditional projections and their Monte Carlo or regression approximations. In the portfolio specialization, only the wealth-relevant blocks of the full tuple are passed to the local solver.

# 5 Numerical Experiments: Adjoint Validation and Constrained Recovery 

We compare three deployment rules. Activation-based DPO deploys the feasible network output directly. B-PGDPO and QP-PGDPO use the same full estimated wealth-relevant adjoint tuple and differ only in the Stage 2 solver: a log-barrier method and an exact quadratic-programming (QP) solve, respectively. The portfolio-only benchmark imposes u := π1: n ≥ 0 and allows the residual cash weight π0 = 1 − 1⊤u to be negative; the cap models additionally impose no borrowing and 0 ≤ Ct ≤ ¯mX t. All adjoints are harvested on the fixed-latent OL-BPTT graph. The shifted wealth-row input is estimated by the anchored nested regression in (47); no reported recovery table fixes this input ex ante. All portfolio blocks are quadratic-affine after the adjoints are fixed, so QP-PGDPO is the exact local-QP reference conditional on the common estimated adjoint tuple. The barrier experiments therefore audit the central path and solver modularity rather than a separate nonquadratic benchmark.

# 6 Conclusion 

We developed a solver-neutral adjoint-to-control framework for constrained continuous-time portfolio choice with wealth and observable investment opportunity factors. A feasible feed-back policy supplies reference rollouts; fixed-latent OL-BPTT then harvests vector first-order and matrix second-order open-loop sensitivities while retaining the state derivatives of a mov-ing feasible-fiber chart. Conditional projection produces the adapted level adjoints, an anchored nested regression estimates the shifted wealth-row martingale input, and a structure-appropriate local solver evaluates the resulting generalized Hamiltonian. B-PGDPO is the smooth log-barrier realization and QP-PGDPO the exact quadratic-affine local-solver specialization conditional on the supplied adjoint tuple. Analytical benchmarks validate complementary parts of the adjoint tuple. In the n = 100 

constrained Merton model, the learned first-adjoint level has mean relative error 0.46% ; un-der the analytical policy, the normalized errors of λX , P XX , and ZX are 0.031% , 0.035% , and 

0.326% . In the predictable-return benchmark, the analytical Brownian coefficient is recovered within 0.260% and 0.986% , and the decoded-policy RMSEs are 2.465 × 10 −3 and 8.429 × 10 −3.The direct P XY –VXY discrepancies, 0.318% and 0.350% , are reported only as numerical diagnos-tics. Across training-budget, consumption-cap, constrained-factor, barrier, and switching-region audits, DPO remains feasible but leaves non-negligible local residuals, QP-PGDPO solves the estimated quadratic-affine blocks to numerical tolerance, and B-PGDPO retains positive mean local generalized-Hamiltonian gains under the common estimated inputs with a small, approxi-mately first-order central-path gap. All final barrier runs satisfy the squared-Newton-decrement stopping rule. The performance-to-adjoint bridge clarifies the role of the reference policy. On a common regular stratum, local quadratic growth converts an εref -optimal reference into O(√εref ) control and first/second-adjoint mismatch. Exact QP recovery does not improve that policy-distance 28 order, but its strongly concave local value loss is O(εref ) after squaring the adjoint-input per-turbation, up to acquisition and numerical errors. This conclusion is local and integrated rather than pointwise, and quadratic growth must be verified rather than inferred from portfolio-block curvature alone. The scope is deliberately targeted. The method avoids a global value-function grid and re-mains practical for large constrained action blocks, but it does not claim dimension-free state complexity or universal dominance over PDE-, BSDE-, or PINN-based methods. The general recovery map retains a nonzero shifted martingale adjoint; no value-Hessian or zero-shift iden-tification is imposed. The fixed-latent CRRA scaling relation is used only as a benchmark wealth–wealth curvature check, while the residual wealth-component shift is estimated directly and ZX is reconstructed only when it is itself of interest. All reported portfolio blocks become quadratic-affine once the adjoints are fixed. Genuinely nonquadratic smooth blocks are covered by the general barrier formulation but are not separately tested in the present numerical study. Pure state constraints, nonsmooth fibers, reflected multipliers, and singular controls require separate analysis.

The QP estimates require a uniform lower eigen-value bound for Q and bounded local Lipschitz constants for (g, Q ). These constants may depend on the portfolio dimension and on covariance conditioning. The framework avoids a state grid but does not imply dimension-free linear algebra.
